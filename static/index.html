Architecture FAQ

Under the hood of the MultiShield framework

MultiShield hosts four neural classifiers locally:

  • Flow Classifier: A 5-layer feed-forward Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) trained on standard UNSW-NB15 connection header profiles.
  • News & Email Auditors: Multi-layer Bi-Directional Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models mapping syntactic weights, vocabulary frequencies, and stopword occurrences.
  • Media Spatial Scanner: A deep Convolutional Neural Network (ResNet-18) evaluating structural face pixel frequencies to detect GAN generator textures.

Evaluating threat sequences on local PyTorch CUDA tensors reduces execution latency to under 15ms. Parallelizing matrix multiplication on local GPU hardware blocks threats inline before packet routing or file system storage actions occur, avoiding high cloud token latency and pricing variables.

The Media Scanner implements Test-Time Augmentation (TTA). Images are scaled, normalized, and evaluated alongside horizontal flips. The resulting probabilities are averaged, using a calibrated decision threshold of 0.4 to ensure StyleGAN facial textures and frequency anomalies are identified with high sensitivity.

While highly optimized, MultiShield has several constraints:

  • Model Drift: The threat models are trained on specific static subsets (e.g. UNSW-NB15, StyleGAN2) and may require retraining or fine-tuning when faced with fresh, zero-day threat formats or adversarial inputs.
  • Sequence Limits: LSTMs for news and phishing email auditing have fixed max-sequence token limits (256 tokens) and may lose context on extremely long files.
  • Face Orientation: The Deepfake ResNet classifier is optimized for centered face portraits and performs best when face detection pre-crops the input images.
